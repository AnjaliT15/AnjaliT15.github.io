About

I am a Research Associate in the Department of Electrical Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Jodhpur. I recently submitted my Ph.D. thesis, Unifying Safety and Robustness for Control of Dynamical Systems under Uncertainty, supervised by Prof. Niladri Sekhar Tripathy.

My research develops unified, optimization-based frameworks for safe, robust, and optimal control. I combine control barrier and Lyapunov functions (CBF/CLF), static output feedback, and reinforcement-learning-augmented optimal control, so that safety, stability, and performance come from a single design rather than being patched together.

Motivation

Drones delivering packages, robots moving through cluttered warehouses, and aerial vehicles inspecting wildfires all share one problem: they must stay safe while their models are imperfect. Uncertainty comes from unmodeled dynamics, external disturbances, and time-varying parameters. Safety comes as constraints: avoiding obstacles, staying within a permitted area, and respecting state and input bounds.

A controller for these systems has to deliver safety and stability together, while the environment imposes state constraints and uncertainty. Only when both sides are handled does the system operate reliably.

Research

My thesis asks one question: how do we design a single safe robust control law that guarantees both safety and stabilization of a dynamical system in the presence of uncertainty? The work progresses through four steps, each adding a layer of real-world difficulty.

  1. 2021 to 2023, Unify

    Unified safe-robust control of uncertain nonlinear systems

    Safety and stabilization can pull a controller in different directions, and designing two separate controllers creates switching problems or conservatism. I reformulated robust stabilization as an optimal control problem using virtual input dynamics, giving one control law with formal safety and stability guarantees under both matched and mismatched uncertainty.

    Safety and robustness should be designed together.

  2. 2023 to 2025, Discretize

    Safety-embedded optimal control for discrete-time systems

    Real robots run digital controllers, and continuous-time safety reasoning does not automatically carry over to sampled control. Using discrete-time control barrier functions, I unified safety, stability, and optimality in one optimization layer without tightening the safety constraints, validated on a single-link manipulator under bounded uncertainty.

    Safety guarantees should follow the way the controller is actually implemented.

  3. 2025 to 2026, Extend

    Safe static output feedback with limited measurements

    Many agents cannot measure their full state. By embedding safety information as a discrete barrier state, I derived safe static output feedback conditions for uncertain discrete-time linear systems, so the controller works from measured outputs alone.

    Limited information becomes a design constraint, not a reason to give up safety.

  4. 2026 to present, Learn

    Safe control meets data-driven learning

    Model-based designs depend on uncertainty models and worst-case bounds, which makes them conservative. I am now integrating reinforcement learning with robust CBF/CLF safety filters, so learned behavior can adapt to changing environments while physical actions stay certifiably safe.

How the safe robust controller works

The controller solves a constrained optimal control problem built from the nominal system, a cost functional, and safety constraints from a control barrier function. A smooth switching function then blends two control laws: a robust stabilizing law u1 far from danger, and a safety-aware law u2 near the unsafe region.

Because the transition is smooth, the system moves between stabilization and safety without abrupt jumps in the control input.

Current work: from safe control to safe learning

My current research moves from the model-driven, certifiable end of the spectrum toward controllers that are explicitly compatible with data-driven decision layers. Each task below adds one new source of real-world complexity.

Navigation among static obstacles

A unicycle robot reaches its goal while avoiding multiple obstacles under unknown, time-varying disturbances, using a learning-augmented CLF-CBF design.

Moving obstacles in unknown flow fields

A robot safely circles a target among static and moving obstacles while an online estimator compensates for an unknown, nonuniform flow field.

Sharing space with a human-like agent

A robot learns goal-directed behavior while a safety filter keeps a minimum separation from an independently moving, human-like agent.

Learning the uncertainty from data

A model of the disturbance and its error bound are learned from interaction data, then used for robust policy design. The aim is to reduce conservatism while keeping safety.

Future goals

Autonomy is moving from systems that follow predefined control laws to systems that perceive, learn, decide, and act in the physical world. This physical AI needs learning and adaptation, but its physical actions must remain safe and reliable.

The question driving my next research

How can heterogeneous human, AI, and robotic agents coordinate safely and effectively under uncertainty while exploiting data-driven intelligence?
